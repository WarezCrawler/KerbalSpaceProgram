# Atmospheric Pressure (atm) by Altitude (KSP 1.9.1 stock)

Companion to the density-ratio table. Cell value = **local static pressure in atm** (Kerbin sea level = 1 atm = 101.325 kPa) - the exact number KSP feeds into an engine's `atmosphereCurve` (the Isp-vs-pressure curve) on that body. An altitude row gives the `atmosphereCurve` x-value; scan a column for a target pressure to find the matching altitude.

## Reading it against Kerbin

The *Kerbin* column is *italicised* (your reference). **Bold** = higher pressure than Kerbin at the same altitude; plain = lower. The bold->plain boundary in each column is the crossover altitude. Comparison is only meaningful within Kerbin's atmosphere (0-70 km).

| Body | vs Kerbin | Crosses Kerbin pressure at |
|------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Jool | higher at every altitude | never (always higher than Kerbin) |
| Eve | higher low, lower high | ~16.5 km |
| Kerbin | reference | - |
| Duna | lower at every altitude | never (stays below Kerbin) |
| Laythe | lower low, higher high | ~21.9 km |

> **Note:** Duna pressure stays below Kerbin at every altitude, yet in the *density* table Duna crosses *above* Kerbin around 29 km - because Duna's cold, heavy (CO2-like) air is dense for its pressure. Pressure and density rank the planets differently.

## Method

Exact stock model (decompiled `CelestialBody.GetPressure`): polytropic `P(h) = P_sl * (1 - L*h/T_sl) ^ k` (kPa), `k = M*g0/(R*L)`; cell = `P(h) / 101.325`. Sea-level pressures: Kerbin 1, Eve 5, Jool 15, Laythe 0.6, Duna 0.0667 atm. Pressure depends only on altitude (not temperature), so unlike density it needs no diurnal caveat.
